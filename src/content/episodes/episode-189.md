**Guests:** Joel Margolis

## TL;DR

- Margolis says HackerOne remains functional, but contrasts its current feel with an earlier era of close community ties and researcher-focused events.
- He argues that platform feedback from researchers and customers needs visible ownership, prioritization, and follow-through.
- He supports using AI as an accelerator while calling for clear, consistent disclosure and researcher choice around report-data use.
- The hosts and guest describe triage as a difficult scaling problem that needs better processes and incentives, not simply more staff.

## Chapter notes

### 00:04:18 · The early days: LHEs, Covid, and the rise of AI

Margolis traces his concerns to the contrast between HackerOne’s early community-oriented period and its present-day experience. He recalls live hacking events as places for researchers to meet and share knowledge, and points to the move online during Covid and the loss of distinctive event details as signs of change.

### 00:17:20 · HSM Program, HAI, and resource allocation

Margolis considers the Hacker Success Member program valuable but says its direct support can give members an advantage unavailable to newer researchers. He argues that experienced users’ feedback deserves a dependable route to product teams, and contrasts long-standing feature requests with useful AI-driven program analytics.

### 00:36:41 · Sales Incentivisation

Margolis questions whether emphasis on sales has outpaced investment in platform development, citing invitations to programs he views as having little research scope. He acknowledges that platforms need revenue, while emphasizing that researchers and high-quality findings are also central to the value customers receive.

### 00:46:10 · AI and Researcher Reports Data

The discussion focuses on inconsistent public messaging about whether HackerOne products use researcher report data. Margolis says the core issue is not simply AI reading reports, but unclear data-use practices; he calls for aligned explanations, advance notice, transparency, and meaningful opt-in or opt-out choices.

### 00:54:38 · How Can H1 Revive its Old Self

Margolis proposes re-centering the company on researchers and customers, with trackable feature requests, timelines, and follow-through rather than abandoning necessary sales work. He also argues that HackerOne should continue innovating and delivering value instead of relying on its established position.

## Key takeaways

- Create a visible feedback process that acknowledges requests, assigns ownership, communicates priorities, and reports back on decisions.
- Treat community trust as a long-term asset: explain product and policy changes clearly, and make researcher participation meaningful.
- Set explicit data-governance rules for submissions, communicate intended uses in plain language, and provide researchers with practical choices.
- Balance customer acquisition with investment in platform reliability, usability, and the quality of programs available to researchers.

## Reference concepts

- **Researcher-centered platform design**: A product and community approach that treats researchers as essential users whose feedback and experience shape platform priorities.
- **Feedback accountability**: A structured process for tracking researcher and customer requests, setting expectations, and communicating what action was taken.
- **AI and report-data governance**: Transparent policies and user choices governing how researcher submissions and their derived information may inform AI-related products.
- **Triage scalability**: The challenge of consistently assessing reports as volume grows, while maintaining clear communication and reducing avoidable back-and-forth.
