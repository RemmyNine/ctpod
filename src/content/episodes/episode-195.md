**Guests:** Frans Rosen

## TL;DR

- AI can accelerate vulnerability discovery and analysis, but similar tools and workflows are also increasing duplicate submissions and pressure on program queues.
- Rosen sees experienced researchers’ contextual knowledge, threat modeling, and ability to assess impact as important differentiators.
- The hosts discuss whether programs should recognize researchers who demonstrate greater impact in previously reported issues.
- Rosen uses AI to support selected parts of his work while preserving the investigation and problem-solving that make hacking enjoyable to him.

## Chapter notes

### 00:02:48 · AI, bug bounty pressure, and researcher value

Rosen compares the current wave of AI-assisted findings with earlier periods of widespread scanning: both can generate duplicates, though he says AI can surface more complex issues. The discussion covers crowded queues, reduced bounty amounts, and the possibility that programs could reward researchers who establish greater impact for an already-known issue.

### 00:30:27 · How Frans uses AI, and where context matters

Rosen describes using AI to revisit his prior reports, look for related behavior, and assist with investigating leads, rather than relying on generic open-ended searches. He and Gardner emphasize that technical knowledge and program context shape useful analysis, while discussing cases such as race conditions and delayed, context-dependent behavior that they believe can challenge AI.

### 00:46:16 · The future of bug bounty and the value of enjoyment

Rosen says he is watching how companies respond to increased submissions, including changes in scope, rewards, and response times, and hopes programs will recognize useful impact analysis. He favors spending time developing a small number of findings over submitting more low-severity reports, and says he continues to use AI selectively while prioritizing the parts of hacking he enjoys.

## Key takeaways

- Programs can reduce duplicate-driven friction by defining how they assess and recognize meaningful new impact on previously reported issues.
- Triage capacity and response times are part of program quality; AI-assisted intake should be evaluated by whether it improves timely, useful review.
- Research quality depends on context and clear impact assessment, not simply on the volume of automated findings.
- Researchers and programs should preserve room for careful validation and learning, especially when AI-generated analysis may obscure how a conclusion was reached.

## Reference concepts

- **Impact escalation**: Demonstrating that a known issue has greater consequences than initially assessed; the hosts discuss whether this contribution should receive distinct recognition.
- **Duplicate pressure**: The increase in overlapping reports when researchers use similar scanners, AI tools, or discovery approaches.
- **Contextual threat modeling**: Assessing security significance in light of a company’s systems, environment, and business or sector-specific expectations.
- **Researcher-AI collaboration**: Using AI to assist with selected analysis while relying on human knowledge to guide questions, interpret results, and judge relevance.
