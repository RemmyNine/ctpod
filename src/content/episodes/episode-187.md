## TL;DR

- Live hacking events remain valuable for motivation and community, though AI-assisted research is changing how participants find bugs.
- When many researchers use similar AI tools, duplicate findings become more common; experience and focused investigation can help differentiate results.
- AI-generated findings still require human review, technical understanding, and clear evidence before reporting.
- The hosts discuss the practical limits of scaling AI research, including model interruptions, compute costs, and the effort needed to triage results.

## Chapter notes

### 00:05:41 · Live hacking events and AI

The hosts consider whether live hacking events remain worthwhile as AI tools let more researchers investigate deeply and increase the chance of duplicate findings. They still value events for the motivation and enjoyment they bring.

### 00:19:27 · Hacker intuition and AI validation

The discussion turns to when researchers should challenge an AI system’s assessment, while recognizing that persistence consumes time and resources. The hosts emphasize that prior expertise can help guide investigation and judge whether a result is security-relevant.

### 00:25:49 · Tool interruptions and resource limits

The hosts share experiences with model-session interruptions and the substantial memory, compute, and subscription costs of AI-assisted research. They discuss efficiency and scaling as practical constraints, alongside the human effort required to review and prepare findings.

### 00:37:00 · Frontier-class vulnerabilities

The hosts reflect on an article about pursuing especially high-impact vulnerabilities and how AI is changing the pace of discovery. They note that understanding and validating a finding can take researchers longer than producing an initial result.

## Key takeaways

- Treat AI-generated findings as leads, not confirmed reports; verify the behavior and its security impact before submission.
- Strong reports depend on a researcher’s ability to explain the issue and support it with evidence, not only on producing a result quickly.
- Plan AI research around the real costs of review, compute, and tool access rather than assuming that more parallel work automatically improves outcomes.
- Live hacking events continue to offer motivation and opportunities to connect with the security community, even as AI changes the competitive landscape.

## Reference concepts

- **AI-assisted security research**: Using AI tools to broaden or deepen security investigations, while retaining human responsibility for assessing the results.
- **Duplicate findings**: Overlapping discoveries that become more likely when researchers use similar tools and approaches on the same scope.
- **Human validation**: Reviewing a candidate finding to confirm its behavior, security relevance, and supporting evidence before reporting.
- **Frontier-class vulnerabilities**: The episode’s term for particularly consequential security issues that researchers are encouraged to pursue.
